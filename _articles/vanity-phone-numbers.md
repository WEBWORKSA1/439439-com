On a phone keypad, 4-3-9 spells HEY. Businesses have used that simple fact for decades: a number that spells a word is easier to remember from a billboard, a radio spot or the side of a van than seven random digits.

## How keypad letters work

Phones follow a standard layout: 2 carries ABC, 3 DEF, 4 GHI, 5 JKL, 6 MNO, 7 PQRS, 8 TUV and 9 WXYZ. The keys 1 and 0 carry no letters. Mobile phones standardised Q on 7 and Z on 9 in the 1990s, when texting made the full alphabet necessary.

Because most keys hold three or four letters, every number has thousands of possible spellings. Only a handful are real words, and finding them is what a phoneword search does. ## Toll-free and local vanity numbers

In North America, toll-free numbers start with codes such as 800, 888, 877, 866, 855, 844 and 833, and the remaining seven digits can spell a word. Toll-free numbers are treated as a public resource: they are reserved through authorised providers, and hoarding or brokering them is not allowed. Local vanity numbers work the same way but keep a local area code, which can feel more personal for a neighbourhood business. Other countries have their own toll-free prefixes, such as 0800 in the UK and 1800 in India.

## How to choose a good one

1. **Keep it short.** Seven letters or fewer after the code. Long phrases are hard to dial.
2. **Avoid ambiguous spellings.** Words with homophones or tricky letters cause misdials.
3. **Print both.** Show the word and the digits: callers on some phones need the digits.
4. **Watch 0 and 1.** They carry no letters, so the word must avoid those positions.
5. **Check it in the culture you serve.** A number full of 4s may spell your brand but put off Chinese-speaking customers. Run it through our [lucky number checker]({{ '/tools/lucky-number/' | relative_url }}).

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Choosing the word before checking availability.** Popular words are often taken; have three alternatives ready.
- **Ignoring the digits people see.** If customers mostly see the number in text messages or on mobile screens, the word matters less than an easy digit pattern.
- **Forgetting about porting.** Make sure the number can move with you if you change providers.

## Is a vanity number worth it?

For businesses that advertise on radio, outdoor signs, vehicles or packaging, a memorable number turns a passing glance into a call. For a business that gets most of its leads from a website, a clean, easy-to-read number may be enough. Either way, it costs little to check what your current number already spells.
