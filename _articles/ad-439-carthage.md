By the 430s the Western Roman Empire was shrinking, but North Africa still looked safe. Its fields fed Italy and its taxes paid for armies. Then, on 19 October 439, the Vandal king Gaiseric took Carthage, the greatest city of Roman Africa, without a siege.

## How it happened

The Vandals, a Germanic people, had crossed from Spain into Africa in 429. After years of fighting, Rome made peace with them in 435, recognising their hold on part of the region but keeping Carthage. Four years later Gaiseric broke the treaty with a surprise attack and seized the city. He made Carthage the capital of a new Vandal kingdom.

## Why it mattered

- **Money and food.** Roman Africa was among the Western Empire's richest provinces. Losing it removed a major source of tax revenue and grain.
- **Sea power.** Carthage gave the Vandals a great harbour. Their ships soon raided across the Mediterranean, and in 455 they sacked Rome itself.
- **A kingdom that lasted.** The Vandal kingdom held North Africa for nearly a century, until the Eastern Roman general Belisarius reconquered it in the 530s.

Historians list the loss of Carthage among the decisive blows that left the Western Empire unable to pay for its own defence. When people ask why Rome fell, 439 is one of the dates on the timeline.

## Meanwhile, elsewhere in 439

- **Roman law.** On 1 January 439 the Theodosian Code came into force. Commissioned by Emperor Theodosius II in 429 and published in 438, it collected imperial laws since Constantine and shaped legal codes for centuries.
- **China.** The Northern Wei conquered the Northern Liang, uniting northern China and ending the era of the Sixteen Kingdoms.
- **The Maya world.** In Tikal, Stela 31 dates the death of the ruler known as Spearthrower Owl, a figure linked to the great city of Teotihuacan, to 439.

## And 439 BC

Go back nine centuries and 439 BC was also eventful. Athens ended a nine-month siege of Samos when the island surrendered, and Roman tradition sets the story of Spurius Maelius, killed on suspicion of seeking to be king, in the same year.
