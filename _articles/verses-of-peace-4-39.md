Chapter and verse numbers were added to scriptures long after they were written, so any overlap between traditions is a coincidence. Still, the coincidence at 4:39 is striking: two of the world's most widely read scriptures place a promise of peace at exactly that address.

## Mark 4:39: peace in a storm

In the Gospel of Mark, Jesus and his disciples are crossing the Sea of Galilee when a violent storm breaks. The disciples, terrified, wake him. In verse 39 he rises, rebukes the wind and speaks to the sea: "Peace, be still." The wind drops and, in the King James wording, there is "a great calm".

The verse is one of the most quoted in Christian preaching on anxiety and trust. The peace here is outward first, a storm stilled, and then inward, as the next verses turn to the disciples' fear and faith.

## Bhagavad Gita 4.39: peace through knowledge

The Bhagavad Gita, part of the Mahabharata, is a dialogue between the warrior Arjuna and Krishna on the eve of battle. Chapter 4 is about knowledge and action. Verse 39 opens with *śraddhāvān labhate jñānam*, "the person of faith gains knowledge".

The full verse names three qualities: faith (*śraddhā*), devotion to the goal, and control of the senses. With them a person gains knowledge, and having gained it soon (*acireṇa*) reaches supreme peace (*parāṁ śāntim*). Where Mark's peace arrives from outside, calming the sea, the Gita's peace grows from within, as the fruit of knowledge.

## Other verses at 4:39

- **Luke 4:39** tells of Simon Peter's mother-in-law: Jesus stands over her, rebukes the fever, and she rises at once to serve the household.
- **John 4:39** records that many Samaritans of the town believed because of a woman's testimony.
- **Deuteronomy 4:39** asks the reader to take to heart that the Lord is God in heaven above and on the earth below, and there is no other.
- **Quran 4:39**, in Surah An-Nisa, asks what harm there would have been in believing in God and the Last Day and giving from what God had provided. Commentators read it as a rebuke of stinginess.

## Why people notice it

Many readers meet 4:39 because the number already matters to them: a time on a clock, a date, a phone number, or a domain like ours. Seeing two verses about peace at the same address is a gentle prompt to pause, whatever your tradition.
