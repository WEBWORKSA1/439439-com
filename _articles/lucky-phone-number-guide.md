A phone number is one of the few numbers you share with almost everyone you meet. Whether you believe in luck or not, the people who call you might, and a number that feels lucky to them is easier to remember and more pleasant to give out. Here is how to choose one well.

## Step 1: decide whose beliefs matter

Start with the people who will see the number most: you, your family, or your customers. A restaurant serving Chinese diners, a jeweller in Dubai and a consultant in Mumbai each need a different number. If your audience is mixed, aim for a number that is neutral or positive across all of them.

## Step 2: know the digits to seek and avoid

- **Chinese audiences:** seek 8 (prosperity), 6 (smoothness) and 9 (long-lasting). Lucky combinations include 168 ("prosper all the way") and 518 ("I will prosper"). Avoid 4, which sounds like death, and 14 and 250.
- **Japanese audiences:** avoid 4 and 9. 7 and 8 are welcome. 39 reads as "thank you", a friendly touch for a customer line.
- **Korean audiences:** avoid 4.
- **Thai audiences:** 9 sounds like stepping forward, a good sign for business.
- **Indian numerology:** add all the digits and reduce to one digit. Many people want that root to match their birth number, the day of birth reduced to one digit.
- **Western audiences:** 7 is classically lucky; avoid 13 and 666.

## Step 3: make it memorable

Luck helps, but memorability pays every day. Look for:

- **Repeats:** 8888, 0000, 1111 at the end.
- **Patterns:** 1234, 4321, 1212, or a mirror such as 1221.
- **Meaningful dates:** a founding year or a birthday.
- **Words:** on a keypad, digits spell letters. Our [keypad tool]({{ '/tools/phone-keypad/' | relative_url }}) shows what your candidate spells.

## Step 4: get the number safely

1. **Ask your current network first.** Many operators sell premium or "choice" numbers through their apps or stores.
2. **Use number portability.** In most countries you can move a number between networks and keep it, so a great number need not tie you to a bad plan.
3. **Check resellers carefully.** A legitimate sale ends with the operator transferring the number into your name. Do not pay in full before the transfer is confirmed.
4. **Keep records.** Save receipts and transfer confirmations; premium numbers can be targets for fraud.

## Step 5: test it

Say the number aloud, write it on a card and read it on a small screen. If it is easy to say, read and dial, and it passes the culture checks you care about, you have a winner.

## How much does a lucky number cost?

Prices range from free (pick a nice number from a network's pool) to extraordinary. Collectible number plates show the ceiling: Dubai's plate P 7 sold for AED 55 million in 2023. Mobile numbers rarely reach those heights, but numbers with long runs of 8s or 9s can command large premiums.
