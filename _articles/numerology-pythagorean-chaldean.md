Numerology is the belief that numbers, and the letters they stand for, describe character and timing. It is not a science, but it is one of the world's most popular ways of thinking about numbers, and its two main systems are easy to learn.

## Pythagorean numerology

Named after the Greek mathematician, though its modern form dates from the twentieth century, this system gives each letter a value from 1 to 9 in alphabetical order: A = 1, B = 2 … I = 9, then J = 1 again, and so on to Z = 8.

The core numbers:

- **Life path:** reduce the day, month and year of birth separately, add them, and reduce again. 11, 22 and 33 are kept as "master numbers".
- **Expression (destiny):** add the values of every letter in your full birth name and reduce.
- **Soul urge:** the vowels only. **Personality:** the consonants only.
- **Personal year:** your birth day plus birth month plus the current year, reduced.

## Chaldean numerology

The Chaldean system, popularised in the West by the palmist Cheiro in his 1926 *Book of Numbers*, assigns values by sound rather than alphabet order. Values run from 1 to 8; no letter is given 9, which was held to be sacred. Chaldean readers also pay attention to the compound number, the two-digit total before reducing, and give each compound its own meaning.

Cheiro linked the digits to the planets: 1 Sun, 2 Moon, 3 Jupiter, 4 Uranus, 5 Mercury, 6 Venus, 7 Neptune, 8 Saturn and 9 Mars. Indian (Vedic) numerology uses the same pattern with Rahu for 4 and Ketu for 7.

Take the name Ada Lovelace, born 10 December 1815:

## Mulank and bhagyank

Indian numerology adds two numbers many people use for choosing names, phone numbers and dates. The **mulank** (root number) is the day of birth reduced to one digit: someone born on the 29th has 2 + 9 = 11, so 1 + 1 = 2. The **bhagyank** (destiny number) reduces the whole date. A common practice is to pick a mobile number whose digits reduce to your mulank or a friendly number.

## Which system should you use?

Pythagorean numerology is simpler and dominates English-language books and websites. Chaldean numerology is older in style and popular in India and among people who prefer compound numbers. Both are belief systems: treat readings as prompts for reflection, not instructions.
