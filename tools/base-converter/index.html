How bases work

In base 10 each place is worth ten times the one to its right. In base 2 each place is worth twice as much, so binary 110110111 is 256 + 128 + 32 + 16 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 439. Bases above 10 borrow letters for the extra digits: in hexadecimal, A to F stand for 10 to 15, and base 36 uses every letter of the alphabet.

Programmers use binary and hexadecimal because they map neatly onto bits: one hex digit is exactly four bits.
